# Chapter 17: Quiz

## 17.1 Go Proverbs

Quiz:

1. Which is better? **Clear.**
2. Which is better? **Copying a little code.**
3. Documentation should primarily be written for... **Users of your code.**
4. Errors are... **Just values.**

Notes:

- The lesson lists Rob Pike's Go Proverbs (https://go-proverbs.github.io/, from his Gopherfest 2015 talk). Each answer is one proverb: "Clear is better than clever", "A little copying is better than a little dependency", "Documentation is for users", "Errors are values".
- Where the course already showed proverbs at work:
  - "Make the zero value useful": `sync.Mutex` and `sync.WaitGroup` need no constructor (14.1). 16.3 shows the opposite case, where the zero `emailStatus` reads as a real state, `EmailBounced`.
  - "Channels orchestrate; mutexes serialize": chapter 13 pipelines close channels down the chain, and chapter 14 guards one map with a lock.
  - "Errors are values" and "Don't just check errors, handle them gracefully": 16.1 wraps with `%w` so callers can inspect the cause with `errors.Is` and `errors.Unwrap`.
  - "interface{} says nothing": 15.8's `[]any` inbox needs a type assertion for every read, which generics then hide behind `Select[T]`.
  - "The bigger the interface, the weaker the abstraction": 15.5's `biller[C]` has two methods, and 15.9 shows that even one generic method stops a type from satisfying an interface.
- "A little copying" in practice: copy a 10-line helper rather than import a module for it. Each dependency brings its own transitive dependencies, licences and CVEs into every firmware or service build. Since Go 1.21, `slices`, `maps`, `cmp` and the built-in `min`/`max` have removed most of the reasons to reach for a utility module.
